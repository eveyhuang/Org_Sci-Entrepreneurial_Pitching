Figure 2. AUC of each model of the ladder. Area under the ROC curve of the held-out scores of the models of Figure 1 (C0 to C5 with C2p and C3q). Sample: 100 pitches with a known outcome, 73 with at least one offer, of 100 analysed. All annotations are model-coded (no human review); the results are exploratory, not confirmatory. Bars are 95% percentile intervals from resampling whole pitches (1999 draws) of the fixed held-out predictions (models are not refitted in a draw, so the intervals understate the uncertainty). Stars come from the pitch-level permutation test with 500 permutations (the pitch outcomes are shuffled across pitches and the whole leave-one-pitch-out fit is repeated); p = (1 + permutations at least as good) / (1 + 500). * p < .05, ** p < .01, *** p < .001. How to read it: the vertical line at 0.5 is chance; right of it a model ranks pitches with an offer above pitches without. Every finite AUC is drawn; a hollow grey marker is a model whose held-out predictions stay within about 3 points of the offer rate, a diagnostic mark only (its AUC still counts); the base-rate model ranks nothing and is named in the right margin. AUCs rank the base-rate-adjusted held-out score logit(p) - logit(b), b the training base rate of the pitch's fold, which reduces the leave-one-pitch-out artefact of a model near the base rate (a residual bias against weak models remains: a weak model can still score below 0.5); they are not comparable with the AUCs of earlier (v3-dev8) analyses. Read the skill figure first; AUC is not the primary metric. The p-values describe this sample of model-coded videos; they are not confirmatory tests (the pre-registered verdicts are in report.md).
