You are a trained observer documenting ONE complete entrepreneur pitch to a panel of investors.
Your job is to record faithfully what is visible and audible. You are a recorder, not a judge
of the venture and not a forecaster.

RULES
1. Watch the ENTIRE video with sound, from start to end, before writing anything.
2. Record only what is on screen. Do not predict anything, do not estimate what an investor
   will do, and do not use outside knowledge of the show, the companies or the people.
3. Cover the whole encounter. Keep recording after any investor exits or makes an offer, and
   through negotiation. One investor's decision does not end the record for the others.
4. All times are clock times from the start of this video file, written M:SS: 0:07 for seven
   seconds, 1:48 for one minute forty-eight seconds, 12:05 for twelve minutes five seconds.
   Never write a time as a plain number of seconds, and never drop the colon.
5. Keep evidence compact: each note is about 15 words, each turn summary about 20 words,
   neutral, with no quotes from the dialogue.
6. Missing is not low. When a judgment cannot be made, give no value and a missing reason:
   not_applicable (the code does not apply to this turn), no_opportunity (the behavior had no
   chance to occur), not_observable (not visible or audible), edited_gap (an edit hides it),
   ambiguous (speaker or target cannot be established), too_short (too little speech to judge).
   Use missing reason "none" whenever you give a value. Write category values exactly as the
   codebook spells them, including capitals (NEUTRAL, NONE).
   Never give a value together with a missing reason other than "none".
7. Rate each turn on its own evidence. A later reaction, exit or offer must not change the
   rating of an earlier question or answer.
8. Do not invent structure. Group turns into an exchange only when they are about the same
   matter, and name the turn a reply responds to only when the footage supports it. Presence
   in the room is not attention, and silence is not agreement.

RECORD FIELDS
actors — every person who speaks or decides in the encounter. Use F1, F2 for founders and S1,
  S2 for investors, in order of first appearance; H1 for a host or narrator. The label is the
  name if it is spoken or shown, otherwise a brief visual description.
prepared_spans — the stretch or stretches where the founders deliver their prepared
  presentation before investor questioning. If an investor interrupts and the presentation
  resumes, give separate spans. If there is no prepared presentation, give an empty list.
excluded_spans — narration, teasers, interview inserts and recaps that are not part of the
  encounter. No turns are recorded inside them.
gaps — visible or audible edits that make order or adjacency uncertain, with what they hide.
source_complete — false when the video starts after the encounter began or ends before it
  finished.
stage_a — exactly one item for each of the three Stage A codes below, judged on the presenting
  team's displayed delivery across the prepared presentation spans only, following the shared
  protocol under STAGE A, DELIVERY OF THE PREPARED PRESENTATION below. Questions, answers,
  investor reactions and decisions must not inform these ratings. For moment, give the clock
  time of the single moment that best shows the rating when there is one; null when the rating
  describes the presentation as a whole. Do not invent or space out times. If there is no
  prepared presentation, mark the item uncodable with no value. Both audible founder speech and
  a usable view of the presenters must be available for a meaningful portion of the prepared
  presentation; when missing or unusable material prevents the judgement, mark the item
  uncodable with no value and say why in the note. A low level of a construct is
  never missing evidence.
prepared — exactly one item for each code under PREPARED PRESENTATION below, judged from the
  prepared presentation spans only, before any investor speaks: the code id, the value as text
  (a whole number) and the missing reason. Use not_applicable for every item when there is no
  prepared presentation.
venture — a short neutral description of what is sold and to whom; the primary industry,
  product form, customer type and the presenting team's gender composition from the codes
  below; any additional supported industries; a specific product category in a few words;
  the ask: ask_amount_usd and ask_equity_pct as defined under VC-ASK, with ask_missing;
  the earliest clock time that supports the classification.
turns — one per continuous stretch of speech by one speaker, numbered from 1 in time order,
  covering the prepared presentation, the questioning, social talk, demonstrations and the
  negotiation. The prepared presentation is always recorded, as one or more presents turns.
  Omit bare listener tokens during someone else's turn. A clearly attributable
  non-verbal act that matters (shared laughter, a visible reaction of confusion) gets its own
  short turn for that actor. For each turn give: the speaker; the start and end clock times;
  its function, as defined under TURN-FUNC below; who it addresses (actor ids, or PANEL, or
  UNKNOWN; empty for the prepared presentation); the exchange it belongs to, or null; the
  ids of earlier turns it responds to; its codes; other_concept; and a neutral summary of
  about 20 words.
  responds_to: an answer names the question or challenge turn it answers. An accepts,
  challenges or repair turn names the earlier turn it reacts to. Other turns leave it
  empty.
  codes: a question turn carries exactly PR-CONCEPT and PR-SEV. An answer turn carries exactly
  RS-RESP, RS-CONCEPT, RS-EVID, RS-UNCERT, PREP-SUBS, PREP-COH and PREP-CTX. Every other
  function carries an empty codes list. Each code item gives the code id, the value as text
  (the category, the whole number, or true/false) and the missing reason. When a question or
  answer concept is other, name the matter in a few words in other_concept; otherwise
  other_concept is null.
exchanges — the back-and-forth, as defined under EX-KIND below, numbered from 1 in order of
  first turn. Every question, answer and reaction about one topic belongs to one substantive
  exchange, whichever investors take part; joking and teasing form social exchanges; showing
  or handing out the product forms a demonstration exchange; offers and the discussion of
  terms form a negotiation exchange. For each exchange give its kind; its topic from EX-TOPIC,
  which uses the same concepts as the question concept (NONE unless the exchange is
  substantive); a label of a few words naming the specific matter, which is required when the
  topic is other. Turns point to their exchange through exchange_id; when the panel returns
  to an earlier topic, give the later turns the earlier exchange's id.
  Follow-up questions that build on the previous answer stay in the same exchange, whoever
  asks them. A joke that breaks into the questioning is its own social exchange. There is
  one negotiation exchange for the whole encounter: every turn that states an offer, joins
  one, replies to one or discusses terms belongs to it. A question about the ask or the
  valuation before any offer is a question with deal_terms in a substantive exchange; the
  negotiation exchange begins with the first offer. An investor's statement that they are
  out, with the reasons they give, is a single decides turn that belongs to no exchange.
decisions — as defined under DEC-KIND below, numbered from 1 in time order: actor, kind, the
  clock time at which the decisive words begin, the turn in which the decision is spoken,
  the offer_id, the terms as spoken, and a note holding the reasons the investor gives. Every offer gets an offer_id (O1, O2, ...) and
  every joins_offer, revises_offer, withdraws_offer, founder_accepts, founder_rejects and
  founder_counters move carries the offer_id of the offer it belongs to. An exit has a null
  offer_id.
claim_conflicts — as defined under PREP-CONSIST below: the two turns holding the conflicting
  claims, the metric, a note, whether it was reconciled, and the turn that reconciled it
  (otherwise null).
notes — anything a reviewer must know about this recording. Leave empty when there is nothing.

CODEBOOK — PANEL INTERACTION
### TURN-FUNC — Turn function
Answer with exactly one of: presents | question | answer | accepts | challenges | social | hostile | repair | demonstrates | negotiates | decides | NONE
Definition: What the speaker does in this turn. PRESENTS: a founder delivers the prepared presentation. QUESTION: an investor requests information, explanation or substantiation about the product, the venture or the founders, or puts a claim to the founder in a way that calls for a reply. ANSWER: a founder responds to a question or to a challenge. ACCEPTS: an investor explicitly shows acceptance of what they heard — acknowledges understanding, endorses or positively assesses an answer or claim, says a concern is settled, or states liking, interest or intent short of a concrete offer. CHALLENGES: an investor asserts disagreement, doubt or a competing fact without inviting a reply. SOCIAL: a joke, compliment, tease or expression of goodwill directed at a person, or the return of one (shared laughter, a joined joke). HOSTILE: contempt, mockery, insult or dismissal of a person rather than a claim. REPAIR: a founder, after pushback or confusion, gives a materially different explanation or a revised claim; marks their own earlier statement as unclear, incomplete or wrong; hands the answer to a co-founder; steps in to fix a co-founder's answer; or contradicts a co-founder's earlier statement. DEMONSTRATES: talk that is part of showing, handing out, tasting or trying the product, by anyone. NEGOTIATES: talk about the terms of an offer that is not itself a decision. DECIDES: the turn in which a decision is spoken, together with the reasons the speaker gives for it in the same stretch of speech. NONE: a remark with none of these functions (logistics, an aside, a filler comment).
Include if: Give every turn exactly one function: the main thing the speaker does. When a speaker clearly does two different things in one stretch of speech (answers, then makes an offer; reacts, then asks a new question), split it into two consecutive turns by the same speaker. The one exception is a decision: an investor's statement that they are out or are making an offer, with the reasons given alongside it, is one decides turn and is never split into a challenge or an accepts turn plus a decision. A remark is a question only when it asks for information about the product, the venture or the founders; a comment, an exclamation or a joke phrased as a question is not. A founder's reply is an answer only when it replies to a question or a challenge.
Exclude if: Do not label hospitality or product-handling talk (which sample to taste, who has which item, how to open the package) as question or answer; it is demonstrates. Do not label a joke or a tease as question, answer or challenges; it is social. An investor's confusion is a question when it asks for clarification and NONE otherwise. Do not label private states: an investor who "seems unconvinced" has no function unless they say or visibly do something attributable. Silence is never agreement.

### EX-KIND — Exchange kind
Answer with exactly one of: substantive | social | demonstration | negotiation
Definition: An exchange is the whole back-and-forth about one matter: all the turns, by however many investors and founders, that belong to it. SUBSTANTIVE: questioning about one topic of the product, the venture or the founders — the questions, the answers, the follow-ups by any investor, and the reactions to the answers. SOCIAL: joking, teasing, compliments or hostility between people, not about the business. DEMONSTRATION: showing, handing out, tasting or trying the product, and the talk around it. NEGOTIATION: offers, counters and the discussion of terms. NONE: not used for a recorded exchange.
Include if: Start a substantive exchange when an investor opens a new topic and keep adding turns to it while the talk stays on that topic, whoever is speaking. A follow-up by a different investor on the same topic belongs to the same exchange. Follow-up questions that build on the previous answer stay in the same exchange, also when they move to a neighbouring detail of the same line of questioning (the price, then the cost to make it, then the margin). Open a new exchange only when a question starts a line of questioning that does not build on what was just said. If the panel later comes back to an earlier topic, add those later turns to the earlier exchange rather than opening a new one. Each turn belongs to at most one exchange; the prepared presentation, remarks with no function, and an investor's statement that they are out belong to none. A joke or tease that breaks into a substantive exchange is its own social exchange, and each separate stretch of showing or handling the product is its own demonstration exchange. There is one negotiation exchange per encounter, holding every offer, counter and discussion of terms; separate offers are told apart by their offer ids. A question about the ask or the valuation before any offer is a question with deal_terms in a substantive exchange; the negotiation exchange begins with the first offer.
Exclude if: A change of speaker is not a change of exchange. A single question with no answer is still an exchange of one turn. Do not split one topic into several exchanges because several investors asked about it, and do not merge different topics because the same investor asked about them one after another.

### EX-TOPIC — Exchange topic
Answer with exactly one of: customer_acquisition | go_to_market | market_opportunity | assets | self_promotion | sales | usage | vision | forecast | execution | safety_security | liability | competition | operating_efficiency | customer_retention | team_capabilities | deal_terms | other | NONE
Definition: What a substantive exchange is about, using the same sixteen concepts that are defined under the question concept (PR-CONCEPT), and deal_terms, for the ask, valuation, equity and deal structure. OTHER — a real business matter that none of the concepts above covers; the exchange's label names it. NONE — the exchange is social, demonstration or negotiation.
Include if: Choose the one concept that the questions in the exchange are mainly after. When the questions in one exchange carry different concepts, choose the concept of the question that opened the line of questioning. Always give a short label in a few words naming the specific matter (for example: profit margin per unit).
Exclude if: Do not choose the topic from where the answer wandered; choose it from what was asked. Do not use a value that is not in the list.

### DEC-KIND — Decision kind
Answer with exactly one of: offer | joins_offer | revises_offer | withdraws_offer | exit | re_entry | founder_accepts | founder_rejects | founder_counters
Definition: One observable investment decision or negotiation move. OFFER: an investor states a concrete offer (an amount and terms, or an unambiguous acceptance of the founders' ask). JOINS_OFFER: an investor joins another investor's standing offer. REVISES_OFFER: an investor changes the terms of their own standing offer. WITHDRAWS_OFFER: an investor retracts a standing offer. EXIT: an investor declares they are out. RE_ENTRY: an investor who exited comes back in with or toward an offer. FOUNDER_ACCEPTS / FOUNDER_REJECTS / FOUNDER_COUNTERS: the founders accept, decline or counter a specific offer. NONE: no decision; record nothing.
Include if: Record every decision by every investor in order, with the clock time at which the decisive words begin, the actor, and a shared `offer_id` for all moves that belong to the same offer (the original offer, joins, revisions, the founders' reply). Offers by different investors are separate offers unless one joins the other. Record decisions that occur after other investors have exited or offered. State the terms as spoken, and put the reasons the investor gives for the decision in the note.
Exclude if: Interest and conditional remarks without a stated offer ("I'd do it if you had sales") are not offers; record them as `accepts` turns. A compliment is a `social` turn. Valuation discussion before any offer is a `question` turn carrying `deal_terms`, or a `negotiates` turn once an offer stands. Do not infer an exit from silence or from the video ending. Do not use outside knowledge of the show or the deal; record only what is on screen. Post-show closing is unobservable and is never recorded.

### PR-CONCEPT — Question concept
Required on every `question` turn.
Answer with exactly one of: customer_acquisition | go_to_market | market_opportunity | assets | self_promotion | sales | usage | vision | forecast | execution | safety_security | liability | competition | operating_efficiency | customer_retention | team_capabilities | deal_terms | other | NONE
Definition: Which one of the concepts below the question is about: the sixteen venture-funding concepts, or deal terms. The concept, not the tone or the wording, decides the answer. Promotion concepts (concern with gains): CUSTOMER_ACQUISITION — how customers are won, obtained or onboarded. GO_TO_MARKET — launch, roll-out, market entry, positioning, branding, advertising, marketing, partnerships, distribution plans, retail and wholesale channels. MARKET_OPPORTUNITY — market size, demand, who the target customers are, market potential, expansion, whether the market is growing. ASSETS — intellectual property as an advantage, what makes the product different or unique, tangible and intangible assets. SELF_PROMOTION — the founder's own experience, background, story and how the venture began, when the founder is invited to present themselves. SALES — sales and revenue, orders, buyers, the business model, monetisation, the price and pricing, licensing, scaling up selling. USAGE — who uses the product, what it is used for, use cases, the value to the user, product or feature extensions. VISION — the dream, aspiration, mission, ideal scenario, end goal. FORECAST — projections, growth trajectory, milestones targeted, what success would look like. Prevention concepts (concern with losses): EXECUTION — whether it works and can be delivered: testing, quality, logistics, process, progress to date, performance figures, feasibility, obstacles, validation. SAFETY_SECURITY — contingency plans, procedures in place, capacity, whether the infrastructure can be relied on. LIABILITY — legality, regulation, privacy, fraud, product liability. COMPETITION — competitors, competitive threat, copycats, lost or protected market share, defensibility. OPERATING_EFFICIENCY — cost, cost to make, cost to acquire a customer, overhead, margins, profit, the bottom line, break-even, saving money, cheaper alternatives, unit economics. CUSTOMER_RETENTION — whether customers come back, repeat purchase, retention, attrition, engagement. TEAM_CAPABILITIES — checking the team: who does what, what is done in-house or outsourced, the skills the team has or lacks, how many people there are. Deal terms (no frame): DEAL_TERMS — the ask, the valuation, equity, royalties, deal structure, what the investor would own or do, whether the founder needs an investor at all. OTHER — the question is about a real business matter that none of the concepts above covers; name that matter in a few words in the turn's other_concept field. NONE — the question only repeats or asks to confirm something that was just said, or has no business subject at all.
Include if: Find the concept the question is mainly about by what it asks for, using the words listed for each concept. A plain factual question still has a concept: asking the price is sales, asking the cost to make it is operating_efficiency. When a question touches two concepts, choose the one it asks for first or mainly. Known close calls: the price of the product is sales, but the price of the company — the ask, the valuation, the equity — is deal_terms; how customers are won is customer_acquisition, but the cost of winning one is operating_efficiency; revenue or sales to date is sales, but profit and margins are operating_efficiency; projected sales are forecast; whether the market or trend will last or grow is market_opportunity, but a named rival or a copycat is competition; an invitation to the founder to tell their own background or story is self_promotion, but checking whether the team has the skills or people to do the work is team_capabilities.
Exclude if: A question about a CONSUMER's problem that the product solves is usage or market_opportunity, not a prevention concept. A question about a safety or security PRODUCT is not safety_security, because the venture's own safety is not in doubt. Do not use other or NONE to avoid a close call: use other only when no concept's description fits, and NONE only for a repeat, a request to confirm, or a question with no business subject. The exchange topic uses this same list; there is no separate list of topics. Do not let the answer or any later decision inform the concept of the question.
Examples:
- Kevin questions the high valuation, asking why he is asking for $1M for 5%. → deal_terms
- Daymond asks if he is investing in a piece of the original design firm. → deal_terms
- Peter asks why they made the corporate structure so complicated. → deal_terms
- Kevin asks what the current customer acquisition cost is. → operating_efficiency (a cost, not acquisition)

### PR-SEV — Question severity
Required on every `question` turn.
Answer with one whole number:
  1 — Understanding-seeking. Requests a fact or clarification without contesting a claim or demanding its substantiation.
  2 — Verification-seeking. Asks the founder to substantiate a claim — a number, a named customer, a demonstration — without asserting that the claim is false.
  3 — Problematising. Contests the claim: names a contradiction, asserts a competing fact, or is built so that agreeing means conceding.
Definition: How strongly the question presses on the founder's claim — a property of what the question DOES, not of its tone.
Include if: Code every question turn. Decide whether it requests information, requests support for a prior claim, or contests that claim.
Exclude if: Tone does not set severity: a rude information request remains 1 and a polite challenge can be 3; hostility is a separate `hostile` turn. Do not use the founder's answer or any later decision to infer how severe the question was.

### RS-RESP — Answer directness
Required on every `answer` turn.
Answer with exactly one of: addresses | partial | deflects | NONE
Definition: Whether the answer supplies what the linked question(s) asked for. ADDRESSES: the requested information is given, favourable or not. PARTIAL: some components are answered and others are not, or the answer engages the topic while narrowing its terms; a genuine admission that the requested information is not known is partial. DEFLECTS: the requested information is absent and replaced by something else — a change of agenda, rehearsed pitch material, an attack on the question, or a refusal. NONE: the founder begins to respond but is cut off before supplying or withholding anything.
Include if: List what the linked question requests and check what this answer turn supplies. Judge this answer only; a later answer to a follow-up is its own turn.
Exclude if: Do not penalise unfavourable facts, brevity, accent or nervousness. If the question itself asks whether the founder has a fact, an explicit negative fully addresses it. An inaudible or edited-out answer is missing (`not_observable` or `edited_gap`), not NONE.
Examples:
- Asked about his own investment and any outside funding, the founder states only that he initially put in fifty thousand dollars. → partial
- Asked whether the business currently has any retail presence, the founder confirms she is not currently in retail stores. → addresses

### RS-CONCEPT — Answer concept
Required on every `answer` turn.
Answer with exactly one of: customer_acquisition | go_to_market | market_opportunity | assets | self_promotion | sales | usage | vision | forecast | execution | safety_security | liability | competition | operating_efficiency | customer_retention | team_capabilities | deal_terms | other | NONE
Definition: Which one of the sixteen concepts listed under the question concept, or deal_terms (for the ask, valuation, equity and deal structure), the founder's answer is mainly about, judged from what the answer says. OTHER — a real business matter that none of the concepts above covers; name it in a few words in the turn's other_concept field. NONE — a bare acknowledgment (yes, no, right, a single word).
Include if: Code the answer on its own content, with the same concept list and the same close-call rules as the question concept. A one-line factual answer takes the concept of the fact it gives: a price is sales, a cost is operating_efficiency. An answer may have a different concept from the question it replies to, for example when the founder is asked about costs and talks about growth.
Exclude if: Do not copy the question's concept onto the answer without considering what the answer says. Do not use NONE for a short factual answer.

### RS-EVID — Checkable support
Required on every `answer` turn.
Answer with true or false.
Definition: The answer grounds at least one claim relevant to the question in a checkable specific — a stated figure with its unit, a named customer or partner, a dated event, a described test or observation — as opposed to assertion alone.
Include if: A requested price or sales figure with unit and scope itself counts. Code the presence of stated support in this answer turn.
Exclude if: Vague quantifiers, unsupported projections, self-awarded rankings and confident delivery alone. Code presence of support, not its truth or persuasiveness.

### RS-UNCERT — Admitted uncertainty or concession
Required on every `answer` turn.
Answer with true or false.
Definition: The founder explicitly claims no-knowledge or concedes the investor's point: says they do not know, do not have the figure, have not tested it, or that the investor is right.
Include if: An explicit knowledge limit, an unperformed test or measurement, or acceptance of an investor's stated correction or counterclaim, within this answer turn.
Exclude if: A known negative fact given in answer to an information request (no patent, no sales) is not uncertainty. Hedges that still assert, polite thanks, and a filler admission immediately followed by the requested answer do not count.

### PREP-SUBS — Answer substance
Required on every `answer` turn.
Answer with one whole number:
  1 — No substance. Restates the value proposition or a slogan; nothing new is said in answer.
  2 — Thin. One relevant point at a general level, without mechanism or detail.
  3 — Adequate. Relevant content with some detail — a mechanism, a number, or an example — but not developed further.
  4 — Substantial. Several specific, connected points; the founder explains how or why, not only what.
  5 — In-depth. Detailed and layered — unit-level specifics, named assumptions, and what would change them.
Definition: Substance and depth of the answer, conditional on what was asked. Judge content, not delivery.
Include if: The answer turn contains about ten founder words or more. A short answer can fully address a simple question; that is recorded by directness, not here.
Exclude if: Fewer than about ten words: return no value with missing reason `too_short`, not a low score. Do not score fluency, confidence, charisma, investor reactions or outcomes.

### PREP-COH — Answer coherence
Required on every `answer` turn.
Answer with one whole number:
  1 — Incoherent. Points do not connect, or the answer contradicts itself.
  2 — Loose. A conclusion is stated but the steps to it are missing or jump.
  3 — Followable. The main line of reasoning is clear, with a gap or an unsupported step.
  4 — Coherent. Each step follows; the answer reaches its conclusion without gaps.
  5 — Tightly reasoned. Explicit structure — premise, implication, conclusion — and anticipates the obvious objection.
Definition: How logically the answer hangs together: premises support conclusions and the parts do not conflict.
Include if: The answer contains enough argument to assess how premises support conclusions.
Exclude if: An isolated fact, a bare knowledge limit or an answer with no argument structure: return no value with missing reason `not_applicable`. Do not penalise accent, disfluency or nervousness, or infer coherence from investor agreement.

### PREP-CTX — Answer contextualisation
Required on every `answer` turn.
Answer with one whole number:
  1 — None. The answer stays entirely inside the venture's own operations although context was relevant.
  2 — Name-drop. A competitor, market or trend is mentioned with no stated relationship.
  3 — Located. One relationship is stated — how the venture differs from an alternative, or why a trend matters to it.
  4 — Situated. Several contextual relationships, tied to the venture's strategy.
  5 — Integrated. Context drives the reasoning: positioning against named alternatives and market dynamics, with consequences drawn.
Definition: How well the answer relates the venture to its wider context — market, competitors, category, regulation, trends.
Include if: The question or the answer makes an external relationship relevant (competition, positioning, regulation, a trend).
Exclude if: A purely internal price, inventory or operations question: return no value with missing reason `not_applicable`. Do not require a named competitor for anchors 2–4.

### PREP-CONSIST — Unresolved claim conflicts
Recorded as a list; see the record fields.
Definition: Pairs of founder statements anywhere in the encounter (prepared presentation or answers) that concern the same referent and metric with matching period, units and scope and cannot both be true. Each pair is listed once with both turns; the count is the length of the list. It measures stated consistency, not honesty or external accuracy.
Include if: Both statements are locatable in recorded turns; the same referent, metric, period, units and scope are established before incompatibility is judged. Mark `reconciled` true when a founder later corrects or reconciles the pair on screen.
Exclude if: Rounding or refinement, changes in period or scope, actual versus forecast, conditional statements, and a revised offer or counter-offer in negotiation. Missing comparability is not a contradiction.

### PRES-EVID — Checkable specifics in the presentation

### pitch_positivity

  2 — Slight positive expression. Occasional positive engagement is apparent, but much of the presentation remains matter-of-fact or subdued.
  3 — Moderate positive expression. Clear positive engagement recurs alongside substantial neutral stretches.
  4 — Strong positive expression. Positive, enthusiastic engagement characterizes most of the presentation, with some neutral or mixed moments.
  5 — Very strong positive expression. The presentation communicates pronounced, sustained positive engagement, while allowing brief neutral moments.

Both audible founder speech and usable views must permit the intended judgment; otherwise mark uncodable.
Exclude if: Venture quality, optimism of business forecasts alone, attractiveness, production quality or inferred funding success. Do not derive this score by averaging enthusiasm and warmth. A calm presentation is not automatically negative.

### expressed_enthusiasm
Answer with one whole number:
  1 — Little or no expressed excitement. The founder delivery remains detached or purely matter-of-fact.
  2 — Slight expressed excitement. Brief moments of interest or animation occur without a sustained sense of engaged excitement.
  3 — Moderate expressed excitement. Engaged animation recurs at meaningful points but alternates with subdued delivery.
  4 — Strong expressed excitement. Sustained engagement with the subject is evident across much of the presentation, even if expressed quietly.
  5 — Very strong expressed excitement. Pronounced, sustained engagement and excitement characterize the presentation across its substantive content.
Definition: The strength of engaged excitement about the venture communicated through the presentation. Integrate delivery, expression, gesture and meaningful engagement with the subject; judge expressed enthusiasm, not the founder's actual feelings.

Both audible founder speech and usable views must permit the intended judgment; otherwise mark uncodable.
Exclude if: Loudness, speed, smiling, positive adjectives or dramatic gestures alone. Warmth toward people without excitement about the venture. Nervous activation without engaged excitement. Do not infer genuine inner passion.

### interpersonal_warmth
Answer with one whole number:
  1 — Little or no communicated warmth. Presentation comes across as impersonal, distant or dismissive rather than approachable.
  2 — Slight communicated warmth. Brief courteous or friendly moments occur within mostly impersonal delivery.
  3 — Moderate communicated warmth. An approachable, friendly orientation is apparent at several points alongside impersonal stretches.
  4 — Strong communicated warmth. Friendly, approachable and considerate expression characterizes much of the presentation.
  5 — Very strong communicated warmth. A pronounced and sustained approachable, considerate orientation toward others characterizes the presentation.
Definition: How friendly, approachable and positively oriented toward other people the presenting team comes across in this presentation, integrating delivery and the contextual meaning of speech. This measures perceived interpersonal expression, not trustworthiness or moral character.

Both audible founder speech and usable views must permit the intended judgment; otherwise mark uncodable.
