# Shared coding protocol: Stage A perceived delivery

Status: provisional research instrument; human validation has not yet been completed.
Use this protocol together with the three-code Stage A perceived-delivery codebook.

Watch the entire supplied clip with sound before assigning ratings. Human coders and
Gemini receive the same clip and substantive instructions, without investment outcomes
or the other coders' ratings. The clip is the prepared presentation, not investor Q&A.

The target is the presenting team's displayed performance over this clip. A solo founder
is a one-person team. For multiple presenters, judge the predominant impression over
founder speaking time; do not let a brief striking contribution determine the score.
Record substantial differences between presenters in overall_notes. These are team-level
judgments, not individual founder scores. Hosts, investors and voiceovers are not targets.

Consider facial expression, gesture, vocal delivery and the meaning of speech together
when relevant to each construct. No channel has a fixed numerical weight, and the channels
need not agree. Explain salient conflicting cues in the evidence. A quiet presenter may
be warm; an energetic presenter may be cold. Do not count smiling or loudness mechanically.
Do not infer genuine inner passion, sincerity, personality, competence or future success.
Ignore attractiveness, accent prestige, production polish, background music, investor
reactions and knowledge of the business or its outcome. Judge expressed presentation,
not the attractiveness of the business idea or its financial prospects.

Use each code's five anchors. Endpoints are allowed when supported, never forced to
achieve a desired distribution. Rate the global positivity item directly, then enthusiasm
and warmth on their own definitions. Do not compute a composite, enforce matching scores,
or use one item's score as the evidence for another.

Both founder speech and usable views of the presenter must be available for a meaningful
portion of the clip. If missing or unusable material prevents the intended audiovisual
judgment, mark the affected code uncodable and explain why. A low level of a construct is
not missing evidence. If a code can still be judged despite partial missingness, describe
the limitation. Insufficient interaction does not itself make a prepared pitch uncodable.

For every scored item, provide a short explanation with at least one clip-relative M:SS
or H:MM:SS timestamp identifying supporting behavior. Cite contrasting moments where
needed to justify the whole-clip rating; do not judge only the most expressive moment.
Evidence records what supports the perception, not a claim about the person's inner state.
For uncodable items, return no score and explain the missing evidence; no timestamp is
required when the whole recording is unusable. Record editing and presenter heterogeneity
in overall_notes. Do not report self-assessed confidence.
